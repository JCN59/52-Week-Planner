## Title: Should You Wait for Lower Rates to Get Pre-Approved? (Read This First)

**Meta description:** Thinking about waiting for rates to drop before getting
pre-approved? Here's why that strategy can cost you the home — and what to do
instead.

---

If you're house-hunting in 2026, you've probably had this thought: *"I'll get
pre-approved once rates come down."* It feels responsible. It's also one of
the most common ways buyers lose out on the home they want.

Here's the honest breakdown.

### Pre-approval is information, not a commitment

A lot of people treat pre-approval like signing a contract. It isn't. It's a
lender reviewing your income, credit, and assets to tell you what you can
realistically borrow — and giving you a document that proves it to sellers.
You can get pre-approved today and still buy in six months, or not at all.

### What waiting actually costs

`[RATE CONTEXT — live market summary inserted here by research]`

- **You miss fast-moving listings.** The right home doesn't wait for your
  paperwork.
- **Your offer looks weaker.** In a competitive situation, a seller will
  almost always favor a pre-approved buyer.
- **You can't act when rates *do* move.** If rates dip, pre-approved buyers
  pounce. Everyone else is still gathering documents.

### What to do instead

1. **Get pre-approved now** so you know your real budget.
2. **Set your target payment**, not just a price — that's what actually
   affects your monthly life.
3. **Stay ready.** If rates improve, you're positioned to move immediately.

### The bottom line

Getting pre-approved early doesn't lock you in — it keeps your options open.
The buyers who win in any market are the ones who are *ready*.
