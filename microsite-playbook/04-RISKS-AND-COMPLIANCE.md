# Risks & Compliance — read before scaling

None of these kill the model. All of them kill sloppy versions of it. Building
this in from day one is also a sales advantage: "compliant lead gen" is a
differentiator when you pitch licensed trades.

## 1. Google's doorway-page policy (the big one)

Google's spam policies explicitly target "doorway" sites: multiple thin sites/pages
funneling users to the same destination. A network of near-identical microsites is
exactly what that policy describes — **if the sites are thin**.

Your defense is real differentiation, which is already in the house rules:
- Each site targets a genuinely distinct sub-niche or market, with content the
  incumbents don't have (published pricing, soil/housing data, cited research).
- Location pages carry real local specifics, not find-and-replace city names.
- If your page is honestly the best answer in the SERP, it reads as a specialist
  site, not a doorway. If you wouldn't show the page to a homeowner proudly,
  don't publish it.

Also: **EMDs are a tiebreaker, not a strategy.** Google devalued exact-match
domains as a ranking shortcut years ago. The domain helps CTR and relevance
slightly; the recon-driven content is what ranks.

## 2. Licensing rules in regulated trades (serious in Texas plumbing)

Many states regulate who may *advertise* trade services. Texas is strict:
advertising plumbing services generally requires a licensed Responsible Master
Plumber, whose **license number must appear in the advertising** (TSBPE rules).
Slab leak detection/repair is plumbing.

Practical compliance for Model 1 (pay-per-lead):
- Partner with a licensed company **before** the site presents itself as a service
  provider, and put their business name + RMP license number on the site; or
- Clearly position the site as an advertising/referral service ("we connect you
  with licensed local plumbers") until a partner is signed.
- For Model 2 (build-and-transfer), this is easy — it's the client's license.

Check the equivalent rules per state and per trade (electrical, HVAC, and roofing
have their own regimes) before launching each site. One paragraph of research per
launch; make it a checklist item.

## 3. Call recording & telemarketing law

- **Call recording:** Texas is one-party consent, but several states (CA, FL, PA,
  WA, IL…) require all-party consent. If you record calls for lead verification,
  play a "calls may be recorded" disclosure — it costs nothing and covers you
  everywhere.
- **TCPA:** inbound calls and form fills you forward are fine. Do NOT sell form
  data to multiple buyers or robo-dial/text form leads without proper consent
  language. One lead → one exclusive buyer, with a consent checkbox on the form
  ("I agree to be contacted about my request"), keeps you clean and is also your
  selling point vs. HomeAdvisor.

## 4. Honesty on the sites themselves (FTC + house rules)

Never: fake reviews (FTC now fines these directly), invented team members, stock
photos captioned as "our crew," fabricated "serving Houston since 1998" claims.
The playbook's "every claim survivable" rule is a legal requirement, not just an
SEO preference. If the site is a lead-gen property rather than the servicing
company, a one-line footer disclosure ("[Site] is a lead generation service;
work is performed by licensed local contractors") is cheap insurance.

## 5. Business risks worth pricing in

- **Time-to-rank:** 60–120+ days is normal. Don't promise clients rankings by a
  date; sell on call logs, not forecasts. Budget for 3–6 months of $0 per site.
- **Algorithm volatility:** any single site can lose its slot in an update.
  The portfolio (many sites, many niches/cities) is the hedge — and the reason
  Model 1 sites that survive are worth multiples of Model 2 build fees.
- **Partner risk:** a partner who answers slowly or does bad work poisons the
  phone number's reputation and your reviews leverage. Month-to-month contracts
  and a bench of 2–3 backup companies per market solve this.
- **Platform dependence:** it's all Google organic. Bing is free extra reach
  (submit there too); a Google Business Profile is NOT available for lead-gen
  sites without a real location — don't fake one (that's how networks get purged).
